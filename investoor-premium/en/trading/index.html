High-frequency trading at the CME

In high-frequency trading (HFT) and algorithmic futures trading at the CME (Chicago Mercantile Exchange), some of the world's leading trading firms are active, particularly in second-by-second order book trading.

These firms use sophisticated algorithms and infrastructure for ultra-fast access to markets such as the E-mini S&P 500, Dow (YM), or commodities.

Well-known firms with algorithms in futures trading at the CME:

  • Citadel Securities
  • Jump Trading
  • DRW
  • Virtu Financial
  • Jane Street
  • Tower Research Capital
  • IMC Trading
  • Hudson River Trading (HRT)
  • Flow Traders
  • XTX Trading

These firms often operate colocated servers directly at the CME to achieve the lowest possible latency. Trading happens in milliseconds or microseconds, especially in index futures like ES, NQ, YM, CL, ZB, etc.

Trading

HFT technologies and strategies

1. Colocation (servers at the exchange)

  • Firms like Citadel or DRW rent physical space in CME data centers to place servers directly next to matching engines.
  • Advantage: Minimal latency, often under 1 millisecond.

2. Real-time order book analysis

  • Algorithms observe every movement in the limit order book:
    • Where are large volumes?
    • Where do orders suddenly disappear?
    • How does book depth change?

3. Strategies

  • Market making: thousands of orders per second on both sides of the book.
  • Latency arbitrage: price differences between futures and indices are exploited in microseconds.

4. Advantages over retail traders

  • Faster order execution
  • Access to raw data feeds (e.g. CME MDP 3.0)
  • Lower fees through high volume
  • Direct infrastructure access without broker latency

5. Disadvantages for small investors

  • Worse fills on market orders
  • Spoofing-like activity can cause confusion
  • Unfair speed — even fast connections cannot compete with HFTs

6. Use long-term strategies

  • HFTs operate in the micro range — swing or day traders can decide on higher timeframes (15m, 1h, 4h).
  • You have time to react instead of losing on the microsecond level.
Trading

Tips for reading the order book

It shows all open buy (bid) and sell (ask) orders with quantities (lots/contracts) and prices in real time.

Identify large orders (walls): Large buy or sell volumes can act as support or resistance.

Absorption: When many sell orders come in but the price still rises, they are swallowed (absorption) – often a bullish sign.

Do you see how many orders are actually executed?

The order book constantly changes – Watch for sudden changes, e.g. when large buy orders suddenly disappear (absorption) or new large sell orders appear.

Use the order book together with charts – only this way do you get reliable trading entries.
